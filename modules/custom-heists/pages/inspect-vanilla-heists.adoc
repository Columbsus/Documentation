= Inspecting Vanilla Heists

With the power of the modkit, not only can we place game assets directly into our editor with full 
functionality and materials, we can also inspect vanilla heists to see what exactly a specific asset name is called.

Inspecting these heists is also an important aspect to get a good insight into how the heists themselves work via seeing what variables they have on certain assets.

[IMPORTANT]
====
This is a highly unsupported action and can cause crashes on many different occasions. 

It is important to save your engine before loading into a vanilla heist.

Level blueprints are not reflected in the modkit. If you try and open the level blueprint, you will crash.

In some situations you may crash when changing levels to and from vanilla heists. 

It is not advised to copy any assets from a vanilla heist into your own heist, or loading them as sublevels. While you may be able to, it is a highly volatile action so tread with caution.
====


== Opening a heist

First step is locating the name of the heist that you want to open, for ease of use I have provided a table of heist names to codename below where you can search them in the /content/ folder to find your heist.

When searching for a codename, you will notice many different types of levels.

[cols=`1,2`]
|===
|Level name |Description

|Base level with no prefix or postfix
|This is the main level that also holds all of the sublevels. This level typically can have any type of asset, but usually will hold the navigation data.

|E_*
|These are the environment sublevels, containing mostly meshes and other similar assets to that. They hold no functionality or lighting typically.

|A_*
|These are the audio sublevels where all of the ambience lives. This is not where the shade or heister voicelines are reflected, those are in the scripting levels.

|L_*
|All of the lighting that isnt directly scripted by events is held in here, as well as post processing volumes, lightmap density, and visiblity volumes (not to be confused with SBZVisibility).

|R_*
|Randomized sublevels. These are small instances that can be randomly moved between locations, such as the bathroom & utility closet in No Rest For the Wicked.

|C_*
|Cinematics. These typically hold the intro camera sequence for the Heist.

|VFX_*
|The special effects displayed in the level such as flashing lights, scammpering rats, and other niagara based assets.

|G_*
|The gameplay sublevels that have various kinds

|G_*_Collision
|This holds mainly blocking volumes & the edge of the map fade to black blueprint.

|G_*_Coverpoints
|This level holds all of the AI navigation coverpoints in the level.

|G_*_Navlinks
|The level that holds all of the special animations that Cops do, such as cloakers running on walls, cops climping over props or other objects, and more.

|G_*_Scripting
|The main scripting of the map, as well as any asset in the heist that the scripting interacts with (ie, enemy spawns, doors, randomization, etc)

|G_*_LivingWorld
|This level refers to the traffic, cars, and wandering civilians of the level. This does not include animations which is in the scripting sublevel.
|===

It is best to click on the main level with no prefix', this will load all sublevels as well so you can see all assets loading in the heist itself.

After you've loaded into the heist, you may notice the level is black and nothing is loaded. 

To Fix this simply click in the levels panel, typically located in the top right, then right click a level and under `Visibility In Editor` hit `hide all` then `show all`.

You are now free to inspect and move around the level!

[TIP]
====
Press `G` in engine to hide all Editor only assets such as volumes, billboards, and more. This can help with performance.
====
